Add-on — strongly recommended

The decision most people make too late.

The Beckham Law election is one of the most significant financial decisions you will make in your first six months in Spain. The window is six months from the date you register as a Spanish tax resident. Miss it and the option is gone permanently.

Beckham Law24%Flat rate on Spanish-source income for six years — vs. progressive rates reaching 47% under standard residency

One decision. Six years. Potentially six figures.

Under standard Spanish tax rules, residents pay progressive income tax on worldwide income. Under Beckham Law, new residents pay a flat 24% on Spanish-source income only. Foreign income — pensions, rental income from properties retained abroad, investment income held outside Spain — is generally excluded from Spanish taxation during the six-year period.

For a client with combined household income of €80,000 — pension, investment draw, rental income from a retained Canadian or US property — the difference between Beckham and standard residency taxation over six years can exceed €60,000. Often significantly more.

We coordinate the election through our tax law partner, who handles the Modelo 149 filing and advises on the income structuring questions that arise before and during the election period.

Common questions

What people ask first.

This isn't a sales call. It's an eligibility assessment — 60 minutes to understand your financial profile, your timeline, and whether the Non-Lucrative Visa is the right path for your situation. If Spain works for you, you'll know it by the end of the call.

What is the Non-Lucrative Visa?
Spain's residency visa for financially independent people — typically retirees, people drawing passive income, or early retirees with sufficient pension or investment income. You are not permitted to work in Spain under this visa, but you may receive pension income, investment income, and dividends from assets held abroad. It is the correct vehicle for the vast majority of North Americans retiring to Spain.
What financial requirements does the NLV have?
The Spanish consulate requires evidence of sufficient passive income to support yourself without working in Spain. The threshold is set by the Spanish government and reviewed periodically. As a general reference point, it has historically been approximately €27,000–€30,000 per year for a single applicant, with additional amounts per dependent — but this changes, and your specific consulate may have additional documentation requirements. We confirm the current figure and how to document your income correctly at the eligibility call.
Can I work in Spain on this visa?
No. The Non-Lucrative Visa is for people who do not need to work in Spain. You may receive income from sources abroad — pensions, investment accounts, rental income from retained properties. You may not take employment or active business income from Spanish sources. If you are planning to work remotely for a non-Spanish company, the Digital Nomad Visa is a more appropriate vehicle — we can advise on this at the eligibility call, though it falls outside our primary service area.
What if my NLV application is refused?
It is uncommon when the application is properly prepared. Refusals typically result from documentation errors, financial threshold presentation issues, or consulate-specific requirements that were not anticipated — the kinds of problems our pre-submission review is designed to catch before the application is filed. If a refusal does occur, we work with our legal partner to understand the grounds and advise on the correct response, whether that is an appeal, a reapplication, or an alternative approach. We do not walk away at the first obstacle.
When should I start this process?
Earlier than you think. The NLV process — from document gathering to consulate appointment to visa issuance — typically takes four to six months depending on your consulate and jurisdiction. If you have a target arrival date in mind, work backwards from it. The eligibility call is the right starting point and costs nothing. You do not need to be ready to proceed immediately to book one.

A gestor processes paperwork. We manage the outcome. The legal partner fee is included in our package — so the advisory and coordination layer costs a single applicant €2,000 above that.
